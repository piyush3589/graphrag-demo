# Basel III and Regulatory Capital

Basel III is an international regulatory framework, developed by the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, that sets minimum Capital and liquidity requirements for banks. It was introduced in response to weaknesses in bank capital regulation exposed by the 2008 financial crisis.

Basel III requires banks to maintain a minimum Capital Adequacy Ratio, calculated as Capital divided by Risk-Weighted Assets. Risk-Weighted Assets are derived primarily from Credit Risk exposures, with additional weightings for Market Risk and Operational Risk.

Basel III also introduced the Liquidity Coverage Ratio, which requires banks to hold enough high-quality liquid assets to survive a 30-day stress scenario. This requirement falls under Liquidity Risk management, typically owned by the Treasury Desk.

Banks report Basel III compliance metrics to their national regulator on a periodic basis. A breach of the Capital Adequacy Ratio or Liquidity Coverage Ratio requires immediate escalation and a remediation plan, since regulators can restrict a bank's operations — including dividend payments and new Transaction approvals — until compliance is restored.
