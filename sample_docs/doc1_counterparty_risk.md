# Counterparty Risk and Transaction Monitoring

A Counterparty is any external party a bank transacts with — another bank, a corporate client, or a government entity. Every Transaction is linked to a Counterparty, and each Counterparty is assigned a Risk Rating (Low, Medium, High) based on factors such as credit history, jurisdiction, and industry sector.

The Transaction Monitoring System flags transactions for manual review when they breach predefined thresholds — for example, a large payment to a Counterparty rated High risk, or a transaction routed through a jurisdiction on a sanctions watchlist.

When a Counterparty's Risk Rating changes (for example, following a credit downgrade), all open Transactions linked to that Counterparty are automatically re-evaluated under Credit Risk policy. This re-evaluation may trigger additional collateral requirements or a reduction in the Counterparty's approved exposure limit.

Counterparty Risk is one of several categories monitored under the bank's broader Enterprise Risk Framework, alongside Market Risk and Liquidity Risk.
