# Treasury Desk and Liquidity Risk

The Treasury Desk is responsible for managing a bank's overall funding, liquidity position, and interest rate exposure. It ensures the bank always holds enough cash and high-quality liquid assets to meet obligations as they fall due — this is the core of Liquidity Risk management.

Liquidity Risk arises when a bank cannot meet short-term obligations without incurring unacceptable losses, for example if too many Counterparties draw on committed credit lines at once, or if a large Transaction settlement falls on a day when liquid asset buffers are already low.

The Treasury Desk monitors the Liquidity Coverage Ratio daily, a metric mandated under Basel III, and maintains a buffer of high-quality liquid assets above the regulatory minimum. It also runs stress tests simulating scenarios such as a sudden Counterparty default or a market-wide funding freeze.

Liquidity Risk is closely linked to Credit Risk: a wave of Counterparty defaults not only creates direct losses but can also drain the liquid asset buffers the Treasury Desk relies on, creating a Liquidity Risk event triggered by a Credit Risk event.
