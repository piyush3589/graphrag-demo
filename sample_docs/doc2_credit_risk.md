# Credit Risk

Credit Risk is the risk that a Counterparty fails to meet its payment obligations on a Transaction. Banks manage Credit Risk by setting an Exposure Limit per Counterparty — the maximum amount the bank is willing to have outstanding with that party at any time.

Credit Risk is measured using Probability of Default (PD), Loss Given Default (LGD), and Exposure at Default (EAD). These three figures combine to produce an Expected Loss estimate for each Counterparty relationship.

Under the Basel III regulatory framework, banks must hold a minimum level of Capital proportional to their Risk-Weighted Assets, which are calculated directly from Credit Risk exposures across all Counterparties. A Counterparty with a High Risk Rating contributes more heavily to Risk-Weighted Assets, increasing the Capital the bank must hold against it.

Credit Risk sits within the Enterprise Risk Framework alongside Market Risk and Liquidity Risk, and is typically the largest single risk category for a commercial bank's Treasury Desk to manage.
